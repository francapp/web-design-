LATAM XO · 2023 — 2024

Building your app has never been this fast

The brand and modular platform that let any company launch their crypto, banking, or fan engagement app without building everything from scratch.

Role
UX/UI Designer Sr.
Company
Latam XO
Tools
Figma · Illustrator · Photoshop

The problem

Building a blockchain product from scratch is slow and expensive: every company that wants to launch its own crypto, banking, or fan engagement app ends up reinventing the same pieces — wallet, payments, authentication — before it can show anything to the market. LatamXO needed a brand and a product that communicated exactly the opposite of that friction: that it's possible to go to market fast without sacrificing a reliable, proprietary product.

Thinking the product, not just the interface

Before designing any screen I took part in conversations about what the platform should offer as a product: which verticals made the most sense to start with — wallet, crypto chat, NFC tickets, asset tokenization —, in what order to build them, and what they needed to share so a client could combine them without it feeling like different products held together with tape. That product definition is what I later translated into the modular catalog and the design system that unifies all the pieces.

Brand and positioning

I defined LatamXO's identity around a simple promise — "Build fast. Your own app." — : it's not a generic template, it's a modular platform each client assembles to their own specifications. The brand work had to hold two things at once: the technical credibility a financial and blockchain ecosystem demands, and the speed of a plug & play product.

A white-label solution catalog

Instead of selling "an app," LatamXO offers a catalog of solutions a client can combine: wallets, crypto chat, NFC tickets, asset tokenization, and other verticals like rewards, gaming, and community. I designed the web section that communicates that catalog as a menu of modules, not a list of loose features.

The product system

I focused on making the different modules feel like part of one system, even though they solved very different things: the same components, the same hierarchy, and the same vocabulary across the four verticals I designed.

Crypto chat

I designed a chat where users can send money, crypto, or NFTs within the same conversation, without leaving the app.

Wallet

I designed the wallet with multi-currency balance (BTC, USDT, ARS) and integrated exchange, with primary actions always one tap away.

NFC tickets

I designed event tickets with QR code and NFC, built for resale and door-side verification.

Asset tokenization

I built the investment module for tokenized real-world assets — like properties — with rental income tracking and fractional ownership.

The outcome

The platform now underpins more than 120 projects, with over 600M in on-chain transactions processed and more than 100 apps built on the same modular system — from NFT tickets for sports events to games and fan engagement experiences. The design system and module catalog are largely what lets LatamXO promise a development speed far superior to building a blockchain product from scratch.

Takeaways

Designing for a white-label product changes the criteria: it's not enough for a screen to work — it has to keep working when another brand changes the color, the logo, and the name. That forced me to decide what was structure and what was skin, and to solve the system before each individual screen.

It was also the project where I most clearly saw the value of entering the product conversation early. Defining which verticals made sense and what they needed to share was what kept the catalog from becoming a sum of disconnected modules.
